Intel's climate promises, checked

A data case study by Sahar Rao

Intel set five climate deadlines. One is nearly met.

In May 2020 Intel set public goals for clean power, water and waste, then added net-zero emissions deadlines for 2040 and 2050. Below, each goal sits against Intel's own reported numbers for 2021–2025.

Due 2030

100% renewable electricity

One point short: 99% in 2025.

The jump came early, from 80% in 2021 to 99% by 2023. In 2025 Intel reported 100% renewable electricity at its U.S., Europe, Israel, Malaysia, Vietnam and China locations, with Costa Rica at 97%.

Due 2030

Zero waste to landfill

Three times the limit: 3% in 2025.

Intel counts "zero" as less than 1% of total waste going to landfill. The share fell from 6% in 2022 and 2023 to 3% in 2025, while total waste dropped from 344 to 249 thousand tons over the five years.

Due 2030

Net positive water

Over 100% globally, but the goal is counted country by country.

Net positive means water returned and restored exceeds fresh water consumed. The global total has stayed above 100% since 2022, but Intel says the goal is met only when each country hits its own target. For 2025 it reports net positive water in the U.S., India and Costa Rica.

Due 2040

Net-zero Scope 1 and 2 emissions

Rising since 2023: up 48% in two years.

Intel reports absolute emissions 67% below their 2006 peak. Within the reported window, though, emissions fell to 0.89 million tonnes in 2023, then climbed to 1.32 million in 2025, while energy use rose from 10.8 to 12.0 billion kWh. The 48% is computed here from Intel's figures.

Due 2050

Net-zero upstream Scope 3

No progress number published yet.

Upstream Scope 3 covers emissions in Intel's supply chain. The 2025-26 summary reports no figure for it; in 2026 Intel is asking its top suppliers to submit validated reduction goals and plans.

How this was checked

  • Every yearly figure comes from the performance data table in Intel's 2025-26 Corporate Responsibility Report executive summary. Goal definitions come from its footnotes.
  • The 2020 goal date comes from Intel's May 14, 2020 editorial announcing RISE.
  • The only computed number is the 48% rise in emissions: (1.32 ÷ 0.89 − 1) × 100 = 48.3%. Everything else is as Intel reports it, not independently audited.
  • Emissions are Scope 1 plus market-based Scope 2, the method Intel uses in its table.
